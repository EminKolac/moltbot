# The Qullamaggie Playbook

### How Kristjan Kullamägi Trades — A Complete Practitioner's Guide

*Educational synthesis compiled from his blog (qullamaggie.com / qullamaggie.net), his YouTube
and Twitch educational content, his Chat With Traders interview, and curated community notes.
This is not financial advice. His returns are self-reported and were earned largely in
exceptional bull markets — see the Caveats section before risking real capital.*

---

## 0. TL;DR — the whole method on one page

Qullamaggie is a **momentum swing trader**. He buys the **strongest stocks in the market** after
they have made a big move and then paused to consolidate, enters on a tight **opening-range-high
breakout**, risks a small fixed fraction of his account with a **stop no wider than one average
daily range**, sells a third-to-half into strength after a few days, and **trails the rest on the
10- or 20-day moving average** to catch the occasional huge winner. He does this only when the
broad market is trending up; otherwise he sits in cash.

The edge is **not** a high win rate (his is ~25–35%). The edge is **asymmetry**: many small
~1R losers paid for by a few 10–50R winners, compounded with disciplined position sizing.

He uses **three setups**, in order of how often they appear and how early you should learn them:

| # | Setup | What it is | Direction |
|---|-------|-----------|-----------|
| 1 | **Breakout / Continuation** | Strong stock consolidates after a big run, then breaks out again | Long |
| 2 | **Episodic Pivot (EP)** | A quiet stock gaps up hard on a catalyst (usually earnings) | Long |
| 3 | **Parabolic Short** | An over-extended, climactic stock rolls over | Short (advanced) |

> **Where his emphasis is now (per his 2023 streams).** Learn the Breakout first — it is the
> foundation — but be aware that since ~2021 he has traded mostly **Episodic Pivots**, because the
> choppy 2022-2023 tape stopped rewarding breakouts: *"I haven't really traded breakouts since 2021;
> EPs have worked way better... EPs have been 50 times better than breakouts."* He also calls the EP
> *"probably the best setup for most people,"* because you can prep before the open instead of
> watching every tick. But this is **regime-dependent, not a permanent switch**: when the strong
> 2024–2025 momentum tape returned, so did his **breakouts and parabolic shorts** — his own X feed in
> late 2025 is full of base-breakout calls (*$ALAB, $DAVE*) and parabolic shorts (*"$QBTS air coming
> out"*). The lesson isn't "trade EPs"; it's **trade whatever the current market is rewarding.**

He is a **human discretionary trader, not a bot.** The screening and rules are mechanical, but
catalyst judgement, market-regime reading, and conviction sizing are discretionary. (A full
mechanical-vs-discretionary breakdown and an automatable spec are in the companion **Quant Spec**.)

## 2. The philosophy (the part most people skip)

1. **Trade the leaders, only the leaders.** The stocks already up the most over 1, 3 and 6 months
   tend to keep leading. Relative strength persists. Ignore cheap, weak, "due to bounce" names.
2. **Big move → tight pause → continuation.** Institutions accumulate in stair-steps. A clean,
   low-volatility consolidation after a sharp advance is institutions holding, not distributing.
3. **Asymmetry over accuracy.** With a ~25–35% win rate, you only need winners to average several
   times your losers. One 20R winner erases twenty 1R losers and then some.
4. **Risk is fixed and tiny; size flexes.** Each trade risks a small, constant fraction of the
   account. Conviction and liquidity decide *how big*, never *how much you can lose*.
5. **Patience is the job.** "It's 99% waiting, 1% doing." He trades hard only when the market
   environment is favorable — perhaps **20–30% of the year** — and sits in cash otherwise.
6. **Mastery removes emotion.** Psychology problems (hesitating on stops, selling winners early)
   are a *symptom of not knowing your setup deeply enough.* The fix is **hundreds to thousands of
   hours** studying one setup until its behavior is internalized. Then the rules feel obvious.

## 3. The market filter (trade with the tide)

Before any long setup, he checks the **regime** using the major indices (S&P 500 / Nasdaq, via SPY
/ QQQ):

- **Risk-on (trade actively, full size):** index **10-day MA is above and sloping up over the
  20-day MA**, and breakouts in leading stocks are *holding*.
- **Caution (smaller, fewer):** moving averages flatten, breakouts start failing → cut size, raise
  cash, hold only the strongest names.
- **Risk-off (mostly cash / look at shorts):** 10-day below 20-day and sloping down, breakouts
  failing en masse → sit out longs; this is when **parabolic shorts** become viable.

He frames it as a **"green light / yellow light / red light"** system on the index: *"if the 10 is
sloping up that's good, if the 20 is sloping up that's good; one starts sloping down — cautious; two
sloping down — cautious; the 10 crosses below the 20 — not really good."* He also layers a slower
**100-day MA** to gauge the bigger backdrop, and on TradingView uses a simple community indicator
(*"ChiLLaX"*) that just encodes the 10/20 cross-and-slope: *"it'll keep you in the market 80% of the
good times and out 80% of the bad times. It's not perfect and it doesn't have to be."* The exact MA
type barely matters — he switched the daily to a **10 EMA** but says *"you can use the 9 EMA or the
10... in a trending market these moving averages work like magic — they're literally magic lines."*

> The single most repeated mistake he warns about: **trying to force longs in a choppy or
> downtrending market.** *"You can't trade breakouts and EPs in downtrending markets... once you get
> uptrending markets — free money."* And: trading them in bad markets means you *"die from a thousand
> cuts."* The setups don't work when the tide is against them.

## 4. Setup #1 — The Breakout / Continuation (learn this first)

This is the bread-and-butter setup and the one to master before anything else.

### 4.1 What you are looking for
A stock that has made a **powerful move** (often **+30% to +100%+ in the last 1–3 months**, ideally
appearing in the **top 1–2% of gainers** over multiple lookbacks), then **consolidates tightly** —
shallow pullback, higher lows, narrowing range, **volume drying up** — usually **"surfing" the
10- or 20-day moving average.**

> **Timing note:** he finds breakouts work **best when the market is just coming out of a
> correction**, and turn **tricky after the indices have gone straight up for a month** (everything
> is already extended). In those late-stage tapes he leans on **EPs** instead. Match the setup to the
> phase of the market, don't force it.

### 4.2 Find it (scan)
- Biggest gainers over **1, 3 and 6 months** (top ~1–2%). Names appearing on **all three** lists
  rank highest.
- **ADR% ≥ ~5–6%** (enough daily range to produce multiple-R winners).
- **Liquidity:** dollar volume roughly **> $100M/day** (he wants institutional-grade liquidity;
  retail traders can relax this but should avoid truly thin names).
- Consolidation **usually 2 weeks to 2 months** (his blog's words); tighter and more orderly is
  better, and during it the stock "surfs" the rising 10- and 20-day (sometimes 50-day) MA. The
  current week's volume is often **~50%+ below** the prior week's — supply drying up.

### 4.3 Enter it (trigger)
The entry is an **opening-range-high (ORH) breakout** on the day price pushes out of the base:
- Watch the **first 1-minute candle**. Buy as price **takes out the high of that 1-minute bar.**
- Missed it / messier open? Use the **5-minute** ORH, then the **hourly (60-minute)** ORH.
- Want a slower version? Buy the **daily break above the consolidation high** with expanding volume.
- **Volume should expand** on the breakout — that confirms demand.

### 4.4 Risk it (stop & size)
- **Stop = the low of the breakout day (LOD).** Hard stop, in the market — no mental stops. The
  rationale (his words): *"a breakout should work right away — it shouldn't hit the low of the day
  after breaking out. That's why the hard stop at the low of the day protects you from failed
  breakouts."* He enters with **market orders**: *"limit orders are for people who want to miss a
  good breakout."*
- **Hard constraint: the distance from entry to stop must be ≤ 1× the stock's ADR (or ATR).** *"If
  the ADR of the stock is 5%, your stop shouldn't be wider than 5%."* If the LOD is more than one
  ADR away, the trade is too loose — **pass on it.**
- **Risk per trade:** small and fixed — *"usually 0.25–1%; I rarely risk more than 1% of my account
  on any trade."* Earlier, with smaller accounts (under a few million), he risked more — **~0.5–1.5%**.
  Beginners should stay at the low end.
- **Position size** falls out of the math: `shares = (account × risk%) ÷ (entry − stop)`. In
  practice positions are often **10–25%** of the account (typically ~10–15%), capped by liquidity
  and by an overnight single-name ceiling (~30%).

### 4.5 Manage it (take profits, then let it run)
- **After ~3–5 days, or at ~2–3R, sell 1/3 to 1/2** into strength. This locks the trade green.
- **Move the stop to breakeven** on the remainder.
- **Trail the rest on a moving average:** the **10-day** for faster movers, the **20-day** for
  slower ones. Exit the remainder on a **close below** that MA.
- **Exception for explosive, same-day parabolic moves** (a stock up 100–300% in a day or two): *"you
  can't use a 10- or 20-day daily moving average for a stop on these — by the time it gets there it
  gives back all the move. You have to sell into strength."* For fast **micro-cap** runners he may
  instead trail an **intraday** average — *"the 10 and 20 EMA on the 60-minute"* — rather than the
  daily. Rule of thumb: the faster the move, the faster (and more intraday) the trail.
- Do **not** average down, ever. Add only if the stock forms a **brand-new** valid base (treated as
  a fresh trade with its own stop).

### 4.6 Hold time
First slice in days; the trailed remainder can run **weeks to months** in a strong trend. The whole
method lives or dies on letting that remainder breathe.

## 5. Setup #2 — The Episodic Pivot (EP)

A **catalyst gap** that marks the *beginning* of a new institutional trend.

### 5.1 What you are looking for
A stock that has been **quiet / flat / basing for ~3–6 months**, that **gaps up hard on a real
catalyst** — most often an **earnings surprise or raised guidance**, sometimes FDA news, a major
contract, or a sector re-rating — and opens **above prior resistance and its key moving averages.**

### 5.2 Find it (scan)
- **Pre-market and first-15-minute gappers**, especially during **earnings season** (run a
  scan ~2:00–2:30pm the day before / pre-open for earnings winners).
- **Gap of ~+10% or more** on the open.
- **Volume exploding** — on the order of **2×+ average daily volume within the first 15–20 minutes**
  (he wants to see the day's normal volume traded almost immediately).
- **It is best if the stock has NOT already rallied over the prior 3–6 months.** His test: *"if the
  stock has already made a big multi-month move into the gap-up, is it really a surprise to the
  market? It's your job to figure it out."* The cleanest EPs gap out of a long, boring base.
- The catalyst is about the **future**: *"forward earnings is everything — the market is
  forward-looking, it cares about guidance / forward growth,"* not the trailing quarter. A genuine
  5-star EP has **mid/high or triple-digit EPS and revenue growth and a big beat vs expectations**
  (his DRCT example: triple-digit growth, guidance raised from ~$128M to ~$190M — *"an insane raise,
  a huge surprise"* — on enormous volume, out of a multi-year base, +200%).
- A theme can **start** with an EP: *"sometimes a sector becomes hot after the leading stocks in it
  start having EPs."* Keep thematic watchlists (he built a 120-name AI/quantum list a month before
  that theme ran) so you recognize the next leaders early.

### 5.3 Enter it (trigger)
Same **opening-range-high** mechanics as the breakout:
- Buy the break of the **1-minute ORH**; fall back to **5-minute** or **hourly** ORH.
- Price must be holding **above the opening price / VWAP** — strength must be real, not fading.

### 5.4 Risk, manage, hold
- **Stop** at the **low of the day**, again **≤ 1× ADR**; conservative version uses the prior
  close. Same **0.25–1%** risk and ADR-driven sizing as the breakout.
- **Take 1/3–1/2 after 3–5 days / at 2–3R**, stop to breakeven, **trail the rest on the 10/20-day
  MA.** Strong EPs can run **much** further than breakouts — they often mark the start of a months-
  long leg, so give the runner room.

## 6. Setup #3 — The Parabolic Short (advanced — do not start here)

His one countertrend setup, and by far the **riskiest**. He explicitly says to attempt it only
**after years of profitability** with the long setups. Shorting a strong stock is dangerous;
position size is smaller and discipline must be perfect.

### 6.1 What you are looking for
A stock in an **unsustainable, near-vertical "parabolic" run** that is showing **exhaustion**:
- Large-cap **up ~50–100%+ in days/weeks**; small-cap **up ~300–1000%+**.
- **3–5+ straight up days**, accelerating, often climaxing on a **huge-range / gap day**.
- **Exhaustion tells:** new highs on **declining** volume, a **climactic** wide bar, then failure to
  hold the early high.

### 6.2 Enter it (trigger) — wait for the roll-over, don't pick the top
- **Opening-range-low (ORL) break:** short when price breaks **below** the first 1- or 5-minute bar
  low after failing to hold strength.
- **VWAP fail:** price bounces into VWAP and prints the first red 1-/5-minute candle there → short
  the failure.
- **Lower-high:** on a bounce it makes a **lower high** than the prior intraday peak → short into it.

### 6.3 Risk & manage
- **Stop above the high of day / climactic bar** (or VWAP reclaim) — must be **tight**.
- **Smaller size** than longs; same small fixed risk.
- **Cover into the 10-day MA**, where these moves often find their first real bounce; trail the rest
  lower. Beware overnight gap risk on shorts; use market stops.
- **Patience is the entire edge here:** *"Don't be too early. WAIT for the right setup. Let the
  amateur shorts get run over — that's where your edge comes in."*
- **Expect smaller reward than the longs:** breakouts and EPs can pay **30–50×+** your risk; a
  parabolic short is more like **5–10×**, but waiting only for perfect setups keeps the win rate up.
- **The mirror-image long:** any time a successful parabolic short collapses **50–60%+** in a few
  days, that sets up a snapback **parabolic long** — enter on the opening-range high or first green
  5-minute candle; these can bounce **50–100%** in a few days. Rarer, but worth knowing.

## 7. Scanning & watchlist workflow

**Tools he is associated with:** **TC2000** (charting + custom scans), **Finviz** (quick relative-
strength screens), and a personal **Evernote** library of thousands of annotated before/after
setup screenshots built over many years.

**A practical daily funnel:**
1. **Finviz relative-strength screens** to pull the leaders:
   - 1-week **> +20%**, 1-month **> +30%** (aggressive **> +50%**), 3-month **> +50%**, 6-month
     **> +100%**.
   - Liquidity: **avg volume > 300k shares**, **dollar volume > ~$100M/day**.
   - A *good, tight* leaders scan returns **fewer than ~60 names**, not hundreds.
2. **TC2000 breakout scan** to flag tradeable consolidations today (exact formula in the Quant Spec).
3. **Tier your lists:** a **wide watchlist** (100–200 names of relative-strength leaders), a
   **weekly watchlist** (50–100), and a **daily focus list of just 1–5** highest-probability names
   with entry, stop, and size pre-written **before the open.**

## 8. Risk management (the real engine)

- **Risk per trade:** ~**0.25%–0.5%** of capital typical; up to ~**1%** on best setups. Small,
  fixed, non-negotiable. (Early on, with a tiny account, he risked a bit more; as the account grew,
  risk-% came down.)
- **Stops:** always at a structural level (LOD / base low), always **≤ 1× ADR**, always **hard
  market stops.** Accept them instantly when hit — hesitation turns a 1R loss into a 3R loss.
- **Position size:** derived from risk and stop distance; typically **10–25%** of account per name,
  capped by liquidity. **Overnight single-name ceiling ~30%.**
- **Concurrent positions:** comfortable up to **~15–20**; beyond that it's hard to manage and is
  itself a sign the market may be overheated.
- **Margin/leverage:** uses it **only when already up and "playing with the house's money,"** up to
  roughly **130%** exposure — never the 2×+ that blows accounts.
- **Exposure scales with the regime:** heavy and leveraged in confirmed uptrends; small or flat in
  chop and downtrends.

## 9. Trade management rules (cheat version)

- Enter on the ORH (1m → 5m → 60m), stop at LOD ≤ 1× ADR.
- Sell **1/3–1/2 into strength** at **~3–5 days or 2–3R.**
- Stop to **breakeven** on the rest.
- **Trail on the 10-day** (fast) **or 20-day** (slow) MA; exit on a **close below.**
- **Never average down.** Add only on a **fresh base**, as a new trade with its own stop.
- **No new risk** when the market regime turns against you.

## 10. Daily & weekly routine

- **Pre-market:** scan overnight/pre-market gappers and earnings winners; finalize the **1–5 name
  focus list** with exact entries, stops, and sizes; set alerts.
- **The open (first 1–2 hours):** this is when almost all his entries happen (ORH breakouts). He
  streams it. After the first hour or two, the high-probability window largely closes.
- **Midday/afternoon:** manage existing positions; mostly leave them alone.
- **Post-close:** **journal every trade** (entry, stop, size, result, screenshot), run end-of-day
  screens, refresh the wide/weekly watchlists, build tomorrow's focus list.
- **Weekly:** print or screenshot trades and **review them like game film**, marking what was done
  well and badly to convert mistakes into rules.

He is European/Dubai-based trading the US session, so his wall-clock hours are shifted, but the
structure (prep → trade the open → manage → journal) is what matters.

## 11. Psychology & the "10,000 hours"

- **Study one setup, deeply, for a long time.** He built an **Evernote database over 7–8 years**,
  going back decades across thousands of stocks, with **1000+ hours** invested and a collection of
  **thousands** of annotated before/after setups. Confidence comes from that database, not from tips.
  He says the **EP alone takes 4–8 earnings seasons (1–2 years)** to truly understand.
- **His own biggest weakness is overtrading** — worth hearing from a great trader: *"I overtrade
  every year, every month, every week. I don't have many big losses because I'm good at cutting them,
  but I take half-size trades in bad markets ten times and it adds up. That's my biggest detractor
  every year."* If it's his hardest problem, expect it to be yours; the regime filter exists largely
  to stop you trading when there's nothing to trade.
- **Don't outsmart a clean setup.** A vivid 2025 example in his own words: *"On July 17th I passed on
  the $ALAB perfect base breakout with higher lows because it had gone nowhere while peers and the
  Nasdaq had been ripping for months. Not gonna try to outsmart a good setup again."* A valid pattern
  is valid; "it's a laggard" or "it already ran" are not reasons to override a textbook base.
- **Trade small for a long time.** Consistency at small size first; scale only once profitable.
- **Journal and screenshot relentlessly.** Your own annotated history is the best teacher.
- **Expect to lose most trades.** A 25–35% win rate means **3–6+ losers in a row is normal**, not a
  broken system. Knowing the math keeps you calm.
- **Patience > activity.** The biggest edge is doing nothing when there's nothing to do.
- **Why most fail:** no setup mastery, no written rules, hesitation on stops, averaging down,
  overtrading in bad conditions, chasing extended moves, trading illiquid names, and stops too tight
  for the stock's natural range.

## 13. Caveats — read this before risking money

- **Self-reported, unaudited returns**, earned heavily in the **2020–2021 momentum bubble.** He lost
  an estimated **~$25M in 2022.** The style is **strongly bull-market-dependent.**
- **Survivorship/regime bias:** the method shines when momentum is rewarded and can bleed in chop.
- **Scale & slippage — the "small account edge":** in his own words, *"with a small account you have
  so much more opportunity and bigger opportunities... but once you get good you won't have a small
  account for long, so that edge starts going away — then you trade larger-cap stocks and longer time
  frames."* Many of the explosive micro-cap moves he points to (he often credits his girlfriend's
  small account for catching them) are ones **he can no longer trade in size.** So your results and
  his are not directly comparable in either direction.
- **Return expectations are bull-market-conditioned:** he'll say *"low- to mid-triple-digit returns
  in a bull market with EPs only — absolutely"* — but note the **"in a bull market"** qualifier. The
  same approach bled in 2022. Do not annualize a good year.
- **Low win rate is real:** you must be able to sit through long losing streaks without deviating.
- **Beware repackaged "courses."** He sells nothing; anyone charging for "his system" is reselling
  free material.
- **This document is educational, not financial advice.** Backtest, paper-trade, and risk only what
  you can afford to lose.

## 14. A 90-day plan to actually learn this

- **Days 1–15 — Study.** Read his three-setups article and FAQ; watch his core videos and a few
  streams. Pick **one setup only: the Breakout.**
- **Days 16–45 — Build the eye.** Pull 200+ historical examples of the breakout (winners *and*
  failures). Screenshot and annotate each: prior move, base shape, volume, ADR, entry, stop,
  outcome. Build your own library.
- **Days 46–75 — Paper / micro-size.** Run the Finviz + TC2000 scans nightly, build a 1–5 name
  focus list, and take ORH entries at **micro size** (risk 0.1–0.25%). Journal every trade.
- **Days 76–90 — Review & codify.** Print your trades, review like game film, and write down the
  specific mistakes you keep making as **rules**. Only then consider slowly increasing size.
- **Throughout:** respect the **market filter** — don't force longs when the indices are below their
  declining 10/20-day MAs.
